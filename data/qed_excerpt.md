# QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter
## Chapter 1: Introduction

What I am going to tell you about is what we teach our physics students in the third or fourth year of graduate school. It is my task to convince you not to turn away because you don't understand it. You see, my physics students don't understand it either. That is because I don't understand it. Nobody does.

Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) describes all the phenomena of the physical world except for gravity and radioactivity. It explains how light and matter interact.

Imagine light as particles we call photons. When a photon hits an electron, it bounces off. But we don't calculate the exact path of the bounce. We calculate the *probability amplitude* of the photon going from point A to point B. The strange thing is, to get the right answer, we have to imagine the photon taking *every possible path* at the same time—even going backward in time for a moment—and sum them all up. Nature is absurd, but it works perfectly.
